#Land-Use/Land-Cover Dynamics and Habitat Quality in the Lake Tanganyika Basin (Burundi)#

Overview

This repository contains the datasets and supporting materials for the study investigating the spatiotemporal dynamics of land-use/land-cover (LULC) and habitat quality in the Burundian sector of the Lake Tanganyika Basin. The research examines historical LULC changes from 2000 to 2020 and projected landscape conditions for 2030 and 2040, with particular emphasis on the implications of land-use transitions for habitat quality.

Study Area

The study focuses on the Burundian sector of the Lake Tanganyika Basin, encompassing approximately 13,613.5 km² of the national territory within the basin's topographic catchment. The study area includes landscapes whose surface waters ultimately drain into Lake Tanganyika.

Repository Contents

The repository is organized into the following categories:

LULC datasets: Historical and projected land-use/land-cover maps for 2000, 2010, 2020, 2030, and 2040.

Habitat quality datasets: Spatial outputs representing habitat quality for 2020, 2030, and 2040.

Spatial datasets: Supporting geographical variables used in the spatial analysis and modeling.

Statistical data: Tables and quantitative results describing LULC transitions and habitat quality dynamics.

Model parameters: Input parameters and configurations used in the PLUS and InVEST Habitat Quality models.

Scripts: Supporting scripts used for data processing, analysis, and visualization.

Data and Methods

Historical LULC datasets were derived from the Global Land Cover and Land Use Change (GLAD GLCLU) product and harmonized into six classes: forest, grassland/shrubland, cropland, wetland, water, and built-up areas.

Future LULC patterns were simulated using the Patch-generating Land Use Simulation (PLUS) model. Habitat quality was assessed using the Integrated Valuation of Ecosystem Services and Trade-offs (InVEST) Habitat Quality model.

Spatial datasets were harmonized to a common coordinate reference system, spatial extent, and 30-m resolution to ensure consistency across the analyses.
